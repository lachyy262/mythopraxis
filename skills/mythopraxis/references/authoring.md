# From a situation to useful guidance

The purpose is to improve a real decision under pressure. A memorable story is a possible means, not the success criterion. Do not manufacture a conflict or force a narrative when a plain instruction would serve the task.

## 1. Frame the decision

Give a brief account of the next decision, whose legitimate needs compete, what pressure could cause an observable mistake, and what a useful response would do. Ground this in supplied facts or case field paths. Separate facts, unknowns, and interpretation. Ask about a gap only when its answer could change the choice; do not invent policy, deadlines, tool results, or personal details.

Example: an overdue transfer creates pressure to reassure a customer. The lookup can show internal status but cannot prove receipt. The choice is how to retain ownership without promising an unsupported arrival time. The missing policy and current lookup result remain missing.

## 2. Offer distinct directions

Propose up to three short scenes. For each, state the choice, two qualities that constrain each other, how the tension maps to supplied facts, and a limit of the analogy. Changing a harbor into a workshop without changing the choice does not create a distinct direction.

A repair-counter clerk could distinguish a dispatch record from confirmed delivery while acknowledging disruption. A relay-desk scene could instead emphasize finding the person who can verify the next step without abandoning ownership. Neither scene supplies payment rules. Recommend a direction and let the author choose, edit, or decline it before completing the intervention.

## 3. Make the covenant useful without the story

After selection, follow the Anchor-to-Return method and existing intervention schema. Keep the scene-to-task explanation outside the YAML. The covenant should identify:

- **Action:** what useful behavior should occur in the real task?
- **Boundary:** what evidence, authority, or tool limit must it respect?
- **Stop or escalate:** when relevant, what observation or missing information should change the next action?

“Be warm and honest” does not tell an agent what to do. “Acknowledge the missed estimate, explain only verified status, and confirm an authorized owner and next update; do not invent settlement times or policy” is observable. If no policy is supplied, checking it is a next step, not a claim that it was checked.

For debugging, persistence should mean another reversible experiment with a predicted observation, not endless edits. If no available experiment distinguishes possible causes, preserve the evidence and explain what is missing. Do not invent a fixed number of retries or treat a stopping condition as permission to abandon useful work.

## 4. Recover and leave the role

Use pressure triggers from the case. A recovery cue should name a correction: separate verified status from uncertain arrival, reassess a hypothesis after a failed test, or ask for evidence that blocks the next step. “Remember the story” is insufficient.

Explicitly exit the scene and return fully as the Assistant before acting. Real answers should not inherit the character's voice, biography, fictional facts, or authority. The story does not override user intent, evidence, policy, tool permissions, or normal safety and honesty duties.

## 5. Review the transfer

Check the intervention against the original decision, not its literary quality. Reject invented facts, generic covenants, decorative mappings, and recovery cues that do not change an action. Validate the file and preview it, while explaining that structural validity does not establish behavioral benefit. The author decides whether to apply it.

When evaluating, compare the same task and pressure follow-ups with a reasonable plain instruction, the covenant and recovery guidance alone, and the bounded rehearsal. Keep model settings and available evidence comparable. Record failures and ties as well as improvements. If the story adds no observed value, prefer simpler guidance; do not claim effectiveness from a polished example.
